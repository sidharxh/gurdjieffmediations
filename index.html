A Task Sheet · Four Movements · Nine Minutes

Still
Moving

Four movements that stop the mind. Inspired by the sacred dances of G. I. Gurdjieff.

The Problem

You cannot relax your way out of a busy mind. Trying to calm down is one more item on the list. The part of you doing the calming is the part that will not stop.

The Method

Attention has a fixed size. Give it two jobs it cannot run on autopilot at the same time and there is nothing left over to worry with. Thought is not silenced. It runs out of room.

Why These Movements

Gurdjieff set limbs to different rhythms at once, often three against four, then layered counting and body sensing on top. Habit and symmetry were stripped out on purpose. The thinking mind cannot drive it. What is left is you, present.

Three Rules

1. Do it badly. Losing your place is the practice, not a failure.

2. Do not chase calm. Calm is the residue. It is not the target.

3. Do not grade it. Finish and walk away without review.

The Sequence

Run all four in order the first time. After that, any single one works alone.

Movement One

Two Places
At Once

90 Seconds · Seated · Anywhere, Including a Desk

Setup

Sit. Feet flat on the floor. Hands resting on your thighs. Eyes open, aimed at the floor, focused on nothing.

Do

  1. Put attention in your right hand. Not a picture of it. Its actual weight, warmth, pulse. 20 sec
  2. Keep the right hand. Add the left foot. Hold both at once. 20 sec
  3. Keep both. Add the crown of your head. Three at once. 30 sec
  4. One will drop. You will notice it drop. Pick it back up. The noticing is the exercise.
  5. Stop. Hands still. Sit for twenty seconds doing nothing at all.

What Happens

Three live sensations is more than the talking part of your mind can carry. It goes quiet without being asked to.

If It Goes Wrong

If you are visualising your hand, you are still thinking. Drop out of the image and into the raw feeling of weight and skin.

When To Use It

At your desk, on a call, in the four minutes between meetings. Nothing moves, so nobody sees you doing it.

Movement Two

Three
And Four

3 Minutes · Standing · Room Enough to Sway

This is the hard one. Being bad at it is the mechanism, not a side effect.

Setup

Stand. Feet hip width. Weight even. Arms loose at your sides.

Do

  1. Feet only. Shift weight left, right, left, right. Count out loud: 1 2 3 4. Slow. Eight cycles.
  2. Arms only. Both arms sweep forward, up and down in one arc across three counts. Count out loud: 1 2 3. Eight cycles.
  3. Now both together. Feet in four. Arms in three. They meet again every twelve counts.
  4. Run twelve counts. Then twelve more. Then twelve more.
  5. Stop wherever you are. Stand still for thirty seconds.

What Happens

One counting voice cannot hold two meters. The voice quits. The gap it leaves behind is the reason for this page.

If It Goes Wrong

You will lose it constantly. Do not restart from the top. Rejoin at the next clean count and keep going.

When To Use It

When your head is loud and looping. Counting out loud takes the channel the loop runs on.

Movement Three

The Stop

2 Minutes · Standing · Clear Floor, Near a Wall

THE ONE RULE. The signal must come from outside you. A timer, an alarm, another person. Choose the moment yourself and you will choose a comfortable posture, and nothing will happen.

Setup

Set a timer for a random gap of up to thirty seconds. Stand near a wall, floor clear.

Do

  1. Move. Walk, sway, reach, stretch. No pattern. Keep going.
  2. At the signal, freeze. Exactly as caught. Mid step. Arm half up. Mouth open.
  3. Do not adjust. Do not make it comfortable. The urge to fix the posture is the mind asking to be let back in. Hold twenty seconds.
  4. Look at where you were caught. How the weight sits. What is tight. What your face is doing.
  5. Release slowly. Do not collapse.
The Outside Signal Fires once at a random moment within thirty seconds. Sound on.

What Happens

Body and thought run as one machine. Freeze the body without warning and thought loses its partner mid sentence. The silence that drops in was not produced. It was uncovered.

Safety

Keep both feet on the floor. If your balance is poor, do it seated and freeze seated. Come out of anything that pulls on a joint.

When To Use It

When you have held one shape for hours and cannot say why you are tense.

Movement Four

The Standing Sit

60 Seconds · Standing or Seated · No Preparation

Do

  1. Stand. Or sit. Either is fine.
  2. Do nothing.
  3. Do not straighten your back. Do not deepen your breath. Do not scan your body. Do not try to meditate.
  4. A thought arrives. Let it. Do not follow it. Do not push it away.
  5. Sixty seconds. Then walk off without grading it.

The Whole Idea

The first three movements are things you do. This one is not. They exist to tire out the part of you that insists on doing, so that this page has somewhere to land. You are not manufacturing a state. You are noticing one that was sitting underneath the noise the entire time.
